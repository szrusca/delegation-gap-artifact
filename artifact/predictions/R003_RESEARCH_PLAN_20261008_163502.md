# Research Proposal: Delegation Is Not Authorization — A Formal Foundation and Protocol Analysis for Multi-Hop AI-Agent Delegation

## Problem Anchor
- **Bottom-line problem**: When an action reaches a resource through a multi-hop agent chain (Human → Agent → Sub-agent → Tool/MCP server → Resource), nobody can state precisely *what the verifier can and cannot conclude*: who acted, on whose behalf, within what authority, and whether the action is what the human asked for. Emerging standards (RFC 8693 token exchange with nested `act`, MCP authorization, the IETF OBO-for-agents draft, the WIMSE credential-delegation draft) and academic schemes (AIP, HDP, CAPMAS) are deployed or proposed without a formal account of their guarantees.
- **Must-solve bottleneck**: (i) Existing schemes guarantee *delegation integrity* (authority is no wider than the parent's) but are routinely presented as solving *authorization* (the action matches the human's task). Their LLM delegators can be steered while holding honest keys, a principal type that classic authorization logics and Dolev-Yao models do not capture. (ii) None of the agent delegation standards has been mechanically analyzed.
- **Non-goals**: a new runtime defense evaluated with LLM agents; prompt-injection detection; empirical measurement of deployed agents; GPU or LLM-API experiments; a new token format for its own sake.
- **Constraints**: CPU only (Tamarin and/or ProVerif); 1-2 researchers; about 14 weeks (today 2026-10-08, TDSC special-issue deadline 2027-01-15); target IEEE TDSC (primary) or IEEE TIFS.
- **Success condition**: (a) a precisely defined model with a *steerable principal* adversary, and an impossibility theorem with a rigorous proof; (b) an evidence hierarchy whose predictions (which property each protocol can or cannot have) are **confirmed by mechanized verification** on at least 4 real protocols; (c) at least one concrete attack or misattribution on a standard or proposed protocol, *or* a mechanized proof that its claimed properties hold, plus a minimal fix proved secure.

## Technical Gap
- **The adversary is known; its consequences for delegation stacks are not characterized.** An agent that keeps honest keys but follows attacker-chosen actions is a classic insider / confused-deputy / malicious-but-authorized-principal model. In symbolic analysis it is standard to model it as a signing oracle on attacker-supplied terms. We do **not** claim a new adversary primitive; we call it the *steered agent* adversary for brevity. What is missing is a **systematic characterization of which delegation properties survive it**. ABLP "speaks-for", SPKI/SDSI, RT and the macaroon/biscuit literature, and every 2025-26 agent-delegation scheme, analyze either honest principals or key compromise. Under key compromise everything trivially fails; under honesty everything trivially holds. Two features of the LLM setting make the steered-agent case central rather than exotic: (a) *every* agent in the chain is steerable by any data it reads, so the insider is the common case; (b) the human's intent τ is never a protocol message, so verifiers can check only what the chain encodes.
- **The property names are not new either.** AUTH, NOESC, ATTR and TASK correspond to established notions: authentication / injective agreement (Lowe), authority attenuation (capabilities, macaroons), attribution / non-repudiation (ISO 7498-2), and intent or task conformance (the AEG position paper; SentinelAgent's intent preservation). The contribution is the separation and sufficiency results among them under the steered-agent adversary, and their mechanized instantiation, not the taxonomy.
- **Agent-delegation work is informal or self-verified.** SentinelAgent states that intent verification is infeasible but gives no model separating which properties chains *can* deliver. AIP and HDP verify only their own tokens empirically. The Authorization-Execution Gap paper is a position paper. The only Tamarin study of agent protocols (2609.00060) covers payment protocols, not identity or delegation.
- **Standards are unanalyzed.** Classic OAuth analyses (Fett et al., the FAPI 2.0 WIM work, Bansal et al.) predate RFC 8693 multi-hop `act` chains and agent-specific drafts.
- **Why naive fixes are insufficient.** "Add more caveats" fails when the task's concrete operands (which repository, which file, which recipient) are discovered at runtime, so the human cannot pre-sign them. "Verify intent with an NLI model" is not a security guarantee (SentinelAgent: 13% under paraphrase).
- **Smallest adequate intervention.** One new adversary primitive (the steerable principal), one theorem with its separation lattice, and mechanized checking of real protocols against the lattice.

## Method Thesis
- **One-sentence thesis**: Under a steered-agent adversary (honest keys, adversary-chosen actions; the standard insider model applied to every hop), multi-hop delegation admits a tight property × evidence lattice. Delegation chains suffice for authentication and non-escalation. Attribution additionally requires request binding. Task-conformance on data-dependent tasks is separated from every chain-only view and needs a human task capsule plus operand provenance. Each separation is witnessed by a concrete protocol-level trace, and mechanized analysis places four agent-delegation standards and one academic scheme in the lattice, exposing gaps against their own claims.
- **Why smallest adequate**: one adversary primitive plus one hierarchy explains both the theory and every verification outcome. No new system is built except a minimal fix that the hierarchy prescribes.
- **Why timely**: agent delegation standards are being written *now* (IETF drafts dated Jul–Aug 2026; MCP auth revised through 2026; Okta and Entra agent identity GA in 2026). Formal analysis can still change them, as the FAPI 2.0 analysis did.

## Contribution Focus
- **Dominant contribution**: the property × evidence lattice for multi-hop agent delegation (4 properties × 4 evidence types), whose cells are filled by tight sufficiency results and separations witnessed by concrete traces. The chain-only separation for TASK is one cell, not a standalone headline. The adversary is deliberately classic.
- **Supporting contribution**: a mechanized (Tamarin) analysis of five protocols that instantiates the hierarchy, with discovered attacks and verified minimal fixes.
- **Explicit non-contributions**: a new delegation token format; a runtime agent sandbox; empirical LLM attack success rates.

## Proposed Method

### Complexity Budget
- **Reused**: Tamarin prover (multiset rewriting, unbounded sessions, built-in signatures and hashing); standard Dolev-Yao network; standard OAuth/JWT abstractions from prior WIM and Tamarin OIDC models.
- **New**: (1) a reusable Tamarin rule pattern for the steered agent (signing oracle bounded by received authority), (2) four property lemmas parameterized by evidence, (3) per-protocol models (4 standards + AIP).
- **Intentionally not used**: probabilistic LLM-behavior models, game-based computational proofs (symbolic is enough for the claims), full web-infrastructure models (WIM; too heavy for 14 weeks), and TLA+ conformance checking.

### System Overview
```
T1 (pen-and-paper + Tamarin witnesses)          T2 (Tamarin models)
 steerable-principal model                       P1 RFC 8693 multi-hop (nested act)
 → properties: AUTH, NOESC, ATTR, TASK           P2 MCP auth (PKCE + RFC 8707 + exchange upstream)
 → evidence: Chain, ReqBind, Capsule, Prov       P3 IETF OBO-for-agents (requested_actor, actor_token)
 → theorems: sufficiency + separation            P4 WIMSE credential delegation (DPoP + RAR)
 → hierarchy predicts verdict per protocol  ──►  P5 AIP (Biscuit chain)   [HDP: optional]
                                                  each: lemmas AUTH/NOESC/ATTR/TASK → verified / attack trace
                                                  → minimal fix → re-verify
```

### Core Mechanism (T1)
- **Entities**: human H; agents A_1..A_n; resource R; actions are tuples (op, operands).
- **Steered agent** A (the classic insider / signing-oracle model, applied to every hop): holds an honest signing key k_A, so the attacker never learns k_A. On each step, A's *choice* (requested action; caveats C' ⊆ C for a child; child identity) is selected by the adversary, subject only to protocol syntax and A's received authority. Key-compromised agents are a separate, stronger class used as a baseline.
- **Task** τ: a finite set of *action templates* (op, operand-pattern) that H intends, e.g. `(push, repo=R0, branch=fix/*)`. A template may contain a **runtime hole** bound to a value produced by a named source (e.g. `(comment, issue=#id from tracker search)`). τ is **data-dependent** if some template has a hole.
- **TASK encoding in Tamarin (committed)**: H's intent is recorded as a trace fact `Intends(H, tid, tmpl)` that is *not* a protocol message, unless the protocol carries a `Capsule`, in which case H signs `tmpl`. Runtime holes are filled only by a fact `Produced(src, tid, v)` from a named source rule. The TASK lemma says: `Accept(R, tid, act) @i ⇒ ∃ tmpl. Intends(H, tid, tmpl) ∧ Matches(act, tmpl)` (holes matched against `Produced` facts). Matching is per protocol (each spec represents scopes and operands differently), so TASK lemmas are protocol-specific instances of one template. Negative verdicts are counterexample traces; positive verdicts are proofs.
- **Evidence types**: `Chain` (signed delegation hops with attenuating caveats); `ReqBind` (each request bound to the immediate sender's key and the chain, e.g. DPoP or an invocation-bound token); `Capsule` (a human-signed statement of τ's static part); `Prov` (signed provenance of operands from their source).
- **Properties**: AUTH (every hop in the accepted chain was actually delegated by its claimed principal; injective agreement); NOESC (the accepted action is within the intersection of caveats); ATTR (the principal recorded by R equals the principal that actually issued the request); TASK (the accepted action is in τ).
- **Theorems (targets)**:
  1. Sufficiency: `Chain` ⇒ AUTH ∧ NOESC, with steerable but key-honest agents.
  2. Attribution separation: `Chain` ⇏ ATTR; `Chain + ReqBind` ⇒ ATTR. Witness: a sibling or upstream agent replays a chain-bearing token.
  3. **Separation (Chain+ReqBind ⇏ TASK)**: for any verifier whose decision is a function of the chain view (identities, caveats, request) only, and which accepts at least one honest execution of a data-dependent task, there is an execution with an identical view in which the accepted action is not in τ. The argument is information-theoretic and short; we present it as one cell of the lattice, not as a deep result. Its value lies in (i) the concrete witness, e.g. "H asks to fix the failing test; the tracker search returns issue #12; a steered A2 instead requests a push to repo-Y, within A1's caveat `repo:*`; the chain view is identical to the honest run", encoded as a Tamarin trace on each protocol, and (ii) its tightness: adding `Capsule` alone fixes the data-independent case, and only `Capsule + Prov` fixes the data-dependent case.
  4. Sufficiency of the evidence: (4a, mandatory and mechanized) `Capsule` ⇒ TASK for data-independent tasks. (4b, pen-and-paper only) `Capsule + Prov` ⇒ TASK for data-dependent tasks whose operand sources are trusted; dropping either admits a counterexample. 4b is kept, at reduced cost, so the paper offers a constructive escape rather than a negative-only result.
- **Why this is the main novelty**: not the adversary and not any single theorem, but the *tight lattice*. Every cell is either a sufficiency proof or a separation with a concrete trace, and the lattice predicts real-specification verdicts.

### Supporting Component (T2)
- **Protocol set**: P1 RFC 8693 multi-hop; P2 MCP authorization; P3 IETF OBO-for-agents; P4 WIMSE credential delegation (mandatory); P5 AIP (the single academic representative, because it is the richest chain and the decisive test of whether richer chains escape the TASK separation). **Conditional**: if by the end of W6 the four standards already witness every lattice cell, AIP is reduced to one counterexample trace for the TASK cell. HDP is optional.
- **Per protocol**: a Tamarin model with roles, message formats per spec, an equational theory (signatures, hashes, JWT as signed tuples), and adversary rules for Dolev-Yao, key-compromise reveal, and steerable choice (the agent emits any syntactically valid choice drawn from adversary-provided terms).
- **Lemmas**: AUTH, NOESC, ATTR, TASK, plus executability (sanity) lemmas.
- **Prediction table**: before running, the hierarchy predicts each protocol's verdicts (e.g., P1 nested `act`: AUTH? depends on whether subject_token binds the audience; ATTR fails without DPoP; TASK fails). Mechanized results are compared to the predictions. A mismatch means either a spec bug (attack) or a model bug, and is reported either way.
- **Minimal fix**: for each violated property within reach, add the evidence the hierarchy names (e.g., bind the chain hash into the audience-restricted token, plus DPoP) and re-verify.
- **Why no sprawl**: T2 exists only to instantiate and stress-test T1. Every lemma maps to a T1 property.

### Modern Primitive Usage
- No LLM is used as a tool in the method. The "frontier" element is the **threat model**: the steerable principal is a formalization of LLM agents. No LLM experiment is needed, because the theorems quantify over all agent choice functions, which subsumes any LLM.

### Integration / Pipeline
Spec reading → abstraction document per protocol (explicit modeling decisions) → Tamarin model → lemmas → results compared to predictions → attack traces translated back to concrete message flows → fix → re-verify. Optionally, replay one discovered attack against an open-source implementation (e.g., Keycloak token exchange or the MCP SDK auth helpers). This is CPU only and optional.

### Failure Modes and Diagnostics
- **Drafts are underspecified** → modeling ambiguity. Detect: list every "MAY/SHOULD" choice. Mitigation: model variants and report results per variant.
- **Tamarin non-termination** for unbounded chains. Detect: timeouts. Mitigation: bound chain length (n ≤ 3 hops covers every claim, since all witnesses need at most 3 hops), add source/helper lemmas and an oracle heuristic, or fall back to ProVerif for the affected lemma. Time-box: a model that does not terminate by the end of W6 is reported with a bounded result.
- **No attacks found.** Still a result: mechanized proofs that the claimed properties hold, together with the TASK impossibility, which is protocol-independent.
- **The TASK separation is judged trivial.** Expected, and handled by the framing: it is presented as one cell, the paper avoids "impossibility" marketing (the title and abstract say "separation" or "boundary"), and the weight rests on lattice tightness, the concrete witnesses, and the predictive verdicts on real specifications.

### Novelty and Elegance Argument
The closest works are SentinelAgent (calculus, but its intent result is informal and NLI-based), 2609.00060 (Tamarin on *payment* protocols), AgentRFC (TLA+ conformance, no cryptographic adversary) and the classic OAuth analyses (no RFC 8693 or agent drafts). The adversary is classic (insider / confused deputy / signing oracle) and we say so. The paper's one idea is to **characterize multi-hop delegation by which evidence buys which property under that adversary**, and to show that this characterization predicts the behavior of real agent-delegation specifications. It is one lattice, not a pile of modules.

## Claim-Driven Validation Sketch
### Claim 1 (dominant): chain evidence provably cannot give TASK, and gives ATTR only with request binding; the hierarchy is tight.
- Minimal experiment: proofs, plus Tamarin counterexample witnesses for each separation (theorems 2-3) on an abstract chain protocol.
- Baselines: honest-agent model and key-compromise model vs. the steered-agent model. The classic dichotomy yields "all hold" or "all fail"; the steered-agent model gives the informative middle.
- Metric: proof completeness; lemma verdicts.
- Expected: separations witnessed; sufficiency lemmas verified.
### Claim 2 (supporting): real protocols sit where the hierarchy predicts, and some fall short of their own claims.
- Minimal experiment: five Tamarin models (4 standards + AIP) × four property lemmas.
- Baselines: each protocol's stated security claims (spec text).
- Metric: verdict per lemma; prediction-match rate; number of concrete attacks; verification time.
- Expected: at least 1 attack on a standards draft (likely ATTR or AUTH under multi-hop exchange); TASK fails everywhere without capsule and provenance; fixes verify.

## Experiment Handoff Inputs
- Must-prove claims: Theorems 1-4; Claim 2's prediction table.
- Must-run ablations: steerable vs. key-compromise adversary; with and without ReqBind; with and without Capsule/Prov (on the fix models); chain length 2 vs 3.
- Critical artifacts: the spec revisions pinned (RFC 8693; MCP spec 2025-11-25 or the latest; draft-oauth-ai-agents-on-behalf-of-user-02; draft-sweeney-wimse-credential-delegation-00; AIP and HDP papers).
- Highest-risk assumptions: drafts are specific enough to model; Tamarin terminates on 3-hop chains.
